## What was built
A survey has a title, an introduction, up to 30 questions (a 0 to 10 recommend score, a 1 to 5 satisfaction score, pick-one, free text; each can be required), a thank-you message, an audience, and a way of reaching people: **a personal link by email** or **one public link** (also usable inside another site's page). Free-text answers become a source of their own kind in the review queue, attributed to the person, so a person decides which become requests. Pages: Surveys (list, builder, results) in the workspace, and `/s/{link}` and `/embed/s/{link}` for the people answering. Tables are in migration 35; the code is `internal/audience`, `internal/survey`, the routes under `/v1/workspaces/{id}/surveys` and `/v1/public/surveys/{link}`.

## Gate 7 items

| What | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Targeting: and/or rules against a brute-force reference | GREEN | an audience is a small tree of "all of", "any of", "not" and tests on name, email domain, date added, has an account, account plan and domain, revenue, renewal date (before, after, within N days) and "asked for request X". The real condition (compiled to parameterised SQL) was compared with a plain-Go evaluator that shares no code with it, on **600 random rules over 90 people** seeded with awkward cases (no email, odd emails, mixed case, no account, accounts missing fields, values on both sides of every boundary, a person with several requests): identical every time, and 470 of the 600 selected a proper subset, so the test is not trivially passing. Mutation-checked: an off-by-one on a date, dropping case-folding, dropping the upper bound of "within N days", dropping "not", `>=` turned into `>`, and loosening the email-domain pattern each fail it. A person without an account simply does not match an account rule, so "not" reads the way it sounds. Bad audiences are refused with plain messages (unknown field, wrong comparison, nesting beyond 4, more than 30 rules, long or malformed values, money with more than two decimals); "everyone" must be written down on purpose, an empty audience is never everyone. |
| Response integrity: duplicates, partial responses, replays, bots, rate limits | GREEN | **one finished response per invitation, and per browser on a public link**; a repeat of a finished response (double tap, replay, changed mind) changes nothing and says so; 12 simultaneous submissions made one response and one follow-up job. Partial responses are saved and resumable (the page reloads the earlier answers) and are never counted in results. Required questions, rating ranges, answers that are not among the offered choices, answers for another survey's question, a question answered twice, two fields at once, and over-long text are all refused and store nothing. Bots: a hidden field people never see (filled in = looks like success, stores nothing), a per-link and per-address request limit (429), the shared public-route limit, and a daily cap on a public link (default 2,000 new responses a day; someone already answering can still finish). Links are signed, so an edited, forged, moved-to-another-workspace, over-long or made-up link is a plain "not found"; a link nobody was sent (still a draft) does not open; closing the survey stops answers and the page says so; **"Make a new link" retires every earlier copy of a public link at once**. Limit: a public link cannot truly prove "one person"; clearing browser storage gives a new browser id (the daily cap and request limits bound the damage). |
| Evidence linkage: free text becomes evidence, verbatim | GREEN | a finished response with written answers queues one background job in the same commit; it stores the text (each question followed by the answer) as a source for that person, after the usual redaction (a card number in an answer was hidden before storing), chunked like any source (every chunk is checked to be an exact slice of the stored text at its offsets), and queues the usual extraction, so quotes are checked against it exactly as for any pasted conversation. Running the job twice stores it once. Ratings-only responses queue nothing. Browser test: the written answer reached the results page and the stored source. **Not measured:** extraction quality on real survey answers (this needs a real model run). |
| Sending safety: same approval, suppression and authentication rules | GREEN | invitations are written down first (nothing is sent), shown with the exact email wording, can be taken off one by one, and go only after a person ticks "I have checked the list and the wording" and approves, with the survey open. The send step itself refuses anything not approved by a person (database constraint plus the claim query; mutation-checked: removing the approval condition, the opt-out check, the open-survey condition, the tombstone step each fails a test) and never sends twice (the provider is given a stable key; a job run twice sent once). **An address on the opt-out list is never written to**, whether it was on the list when the invitation was written down (recorded as "asked not to be emailed"), added later through the usual unsubscribe or bounce path (waiting invitations are withdrawn), or added behind our back between approval and sending (the send step looks again). Every email carries the one-click unsubscribe header and link; the link works through the shared opt-out list and keeps working after the person is deleted. If email is not configured the job fails and the invitation stays approved; transient provider errors retry; a rejection marks it failed and stops; a survey closed meanwhile sends nothing. An architecture test fails the build if any file other than the send worker imports the email package, or if the claim query stops requiring approval and an open survey. |
| Privacy: deleting a person | GREEN | each survey says what happens to a deleted person's answers: **removed** (default), or **kept without a name** (ratings and choices stay, free text goes, since it can identify someone). Their invitations are deleted, the link they were sent stops opening, an opt-out fingerprint is kept for sent invitations so the unsubscribe link still works, and the source made from their free text goes with their other sources. Tested for both settings, and that the other person's answers in the same survey are untouched. Deleting a survey removes its invitations and answers. |
| Isolation: cross-tenant suite extended | GREEN | the generated sweeps cover every new route: signed-out callers get 401; a signed-in stranger sees "not found" on every workspace survey route; a viewer is refused (403) on every change; only admins delete. Surveys, invitations, links and audiences from one workspace cannot be read, changed, invited from or opened through another (tested at the service and HTTP levels); the audience engine returns nobody for another workspace's ids and a request id from another workspace selects nobody here. Row-level security is forced on all five new tables. |

Also checked: browser tests at desktop and phone size cover building, audience counting (a bad value explained in words), the invitation list, approval, the email arriving for exactly the approved person, answering in another browser with no account, saving and resuming, required questions, "already answered", a made-up link, results, closing, the public link (one answer per browser, a new link retiring the old one) and the embedded page shown inside a page on another origin (the only pages that can be framed are `/embed/...`; everything else still refuses framing). No serious accessibility findings on the builder, the survey page or the results. `make check`, `make security` and the full browser suite are green.

## Limits (read before relying on it)
- **No email has gone through a real provider**, only the local mail stand-in. Deliverability, the sender domain and real unsubscribe clicks are untested.
- **Public-link limits see the address of the web server**, not the visitor, because the web app calls the API on the visitor's behalf (the same is true of public votes); the per-link limits and the daily cap are what bound abuse.
- Not built: question branching, templates, reminders to people who have not answered, invitations to people without an email address, a script widget (the embed is an iframe), editing questions after the survey has opened (deliberate: answers keep their meaning), sentiment or themes (Phase 8).
- At most 2,000 invitations per survey (narrow the audience, or raise `MaxInvites`).
- Surveys is a plan feature (`surveys` in the plans file); Free does not include it when billing is on.
- The hosted database needs migration 35 before surveys work there.
