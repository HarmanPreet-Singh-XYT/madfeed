# Gate 9 record: support desk and chat

Date: 2026-10-24    Environment: local (Docker Postgres 18, Go 1.27.3, Node 26)
Status: **FIVE OF SEVEN ITEMS ARE GREEN LOCALLY; TWO CANNOT BE MEASURED WITHOUT A REAL MAIL DOMAIN AND PROVIDER.** Not signed off.

## What was built
- **Inbox** (`internal/inbox`, migration 40, screens under Inbox): one shared list of conversations that arrive by **website chat** or **email**, open or closed, with tabs for open, mine, nobody's and closed.
- **Chat widget**: a small script (`/chat.js`) puts a button on your site that opens a chat window (`/chat/{address}`). Visitors write; the team answers; the visitor sees replies within a few seconds (it checks every 4 seconds; there is no live typing).
- **Email channel**: your mail service posts each incoming email as signed JSON to an address per workspace; the team's replies go out by email from the worker, with a short reference in the subject so the customer's answer joins the same conversation.
- **Work on a conversation**: take it or let it go, close or reopen, write a reply (only the team's words, and only by a person pressing Send) or a private note, and **send a customer's message on to the review queue** as feedback from that person.
- **Status card back**: under that message the conversation shows where the feedback stands *now*: being read, waiting for a person to review, or the request(s) it became with that request's **current status**.

| Message delivery: no lost or duplicated messages; SPF/DKIM/DMARC pass | **PARTLY: not measurable here** | No message is lost or duplicated in anything we control: an incoming email carries an id and the same email twice is stored once; a reply is sent at most once (the mail provider is given a stable key, a job that runs twice sends once, a transient failure retries, a refusal is shown to the team with a "Try again" button); chat messages are stored before they are acknowledged. **Not measured:** delivery across real inboxes, and SPF, DKIM and DMARC, because they need your real sending domain and a real Resend account (and a real incoming-mail hook). A page on another site, a look-alike address, the wrong scheme, a made-up, edited or retired widget address, and a switched-off widget are all refused or "not found"; the chat window itself shows "not available" unless the page that frames it is on the list (read from the browser's own Referer; the widget script also says where it is for browsers that hide it). **XSS:** a message containing `<script>` and `<img onerror>` is stored as the words typed and shown as text, in the visitor's window and in the team's inbox (the guard test that the app never writes raw HTML still passes; a browser test sends `<b>urgent</b>` and sees it as text). | Conversion: message to request/evidence; the status card updates | GREEN | A customer's message (not a note, not a reply) can be sent on to the review queue once (twice stores once), attributed to the person if the address matches one, with the usual redaction and extraction. The card under the message was tested through every stage: queued, read with nothing found, suggestions waiting, then linked to a request, and when that request's status changed the card showed the new status. Deleting a conversation also deletes the feedback made from it. |
| Assignment and concurrency: two people reply at once; reassign | GREEN | **8 people pressing Send on the same message at once: exactly one reply goes out, seven are told the conversation changed** (a reply carries the id of the newest message the person had read; if anything arrived since, including a note or a teammate's reply, nothing is sent). The page holds the Send button until the person confirms they have read the new messages (browser test: a visitor writes while a reply is half typed; Send is disabled until "I have read them"). Two people taking the same conversation: one wins, the other is told. Only the owner or an admin lets go of a conversation, and only after seeing who had it. Everything is mutation-checked. |
| Uptime and recovery: kill workers mid-send; restore from backup | GREEN (as far as it can be locally) | A reply is a database row before it is a job, so a dead worker loses nothing: it stays queued. A worker that died *while sending* leaves the reply "sending"; ten minutes later a retry takes it and sends it **once** (tested; a claim younger than ten minutes is left alone because another worker may still be sending). No email set up: replies wait queued, nothing is claimed. The new tables are part of the whole-database backup and restore drill. **Not measured:** a real outage of a real mail provider. |

An admin can delete one conversation the same way. Replies are never sent to an address on the opt-out list (bounced, complained or asked not to be written to): the reply is marked failed with the reason and retrying stays refused. |
| Isolation: cross-tenant suite extended | GREEN | The generated sweeps cover every new route (signed-out 401; a stranger gets "not found" everywhere; a viewer is refused on every change; only admins delete). ## Limits (read before relying on it)
- **Real email is untested** (see the first row). The incoming side is deliberately provider-neutral: your mail service (or a small adapter in front of it) must post JSON `{from, fromName, subject, text, messageId, autoSubmitted}` with `X-Timestamp` and `X-Signature` (`sha256=` + hex HMAC-SHA256 of `timestamp.body` with the key shown in Settings). I did not build an adapter for a specific provider; Resend's receiving feature was not tried.
- A customer's answer joins its conversation by the `[#reference]` in the subject, and only from the same address. Mail clients that rewrite subjects can break threading; message headers (In-Reply-To) are not used.
- Chat is **checked every 4 seconds**, not live; no typing indicator, no read receipts, no sound.
- **No attachments, no canned replies, no response-time tracking, no assigning to someone else** (there is no member list in the product yet, so a person can take a conversation or let it go).
- The "which site shows the chat" check relies on the browser's Referer or the script's own statement; someone calling the API directly can claim any listed site, which is why the limits above exist. Chat messages are not authenticated: the widget address is public by design.
- Viewers can read conversations but not answer them.
- Hosted database needs migration 40 (and 35 to 39).
